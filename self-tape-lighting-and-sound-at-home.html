Self-tape lighting and sound at home with what you already own

You do not need to buy anything to get a self-tape that looks and sounds clean. Most of the difference comes from where you put the light and the microphone you already have, not from new gear.

Start with a window

Daylight through a window, especially one that is not in direct sun, is the easiest light in most homes. Face the window rather than sitting with it behind you, and the difference shows up immediately on camera.

Add a lamp if the light is uneven

A regular lamp, placed roughly at eye height and slightly to one side of the camera, fills in shadows that daylight alone leaves on one side of your face.

Point the lamp at your face, not the wall behind you, and move it closer before you assume you need a brighter bulb.

Avoid backlight

A bright window or a lamp behind you will make the camera expose for the background, leaving your face dark no matter how good the performance is.

If a bright window is behind your only good camera spot, close the blinds partway and light your face from the front instead.

Use a phone as your microphone

A phone propped just outside the frame, recording audio only, almost always beats a laptop's built-in microphone, which sits far from your mouth and picks up fan noise from the machine itself.

If you use a separate audio recording, clap once at the start of the take so the two files are easy to line up later.

Choose the quietest room, not the biggest

A small room with soft surfaces, like a bedroom with a rug and curtains, usually sounds better than a large open room, because hard floors and bare walls bounce sound around and make it echo.

Turn off anything that hums before you start: a refrigerator, a fan, an air conditioner, or a dishwasher running nearby.

Test before the real take

Record ten seconds of you talking at a normal volume, then listen back with headphones, not the room speakers, before you commit to that setup.

Headphones reveal hums and echoes that speakers hide. If something sounds off in headphones, it will sound off to whoever reviews the tape.

Keep the camera steady

A wobbling frame distracts from a performance faster than most people expect. Stack of books, a shelf, or a phone tripod all work better than holding the camera or balancing it on something uneven.

Check the frame is level and the camera will not slide once you start moving or raising your voice in the scene.

Small fixes that add up

A white sheet or poster board held just out of frame can bounce light back onto a shadowed side of your face. A closet full of clothes, left open just out of frame, quiets a room by soaking up echo.

None of this requires new equipment. It requires walking around your space once, camera in hand, before the day you actually record.
