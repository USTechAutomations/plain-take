Self-tape coach or written notes: which do you need?

A coach and written notes do different jobs. A coach is a person working with you in real time. Written notes are a read of one finished tape. Which one you need depends on what is stopping the tape from being what you want.

What a live coach does well

A coach can watch you work, ask what you meant, change one thing and have you try it again straight away. They can know you over time, notice a habit that keeps coming back, and help you understand what a scene is about when you cannot find it. They can also read with you and respond.

When a coach is worth it

  • You are stuck on what the scene means, not on how the tape looks.
  • The role matters a great deal to you and you want to rehearse it with someone who responds.
  • You are new to acting on camera and want someone to show you the craft.
  • You want steady work over weeks, not a one-off opinion.

When written notes are enough

  • The scene is worked and you want to check how it comes across on the tape.
  • You want something specific to fix before you send it tonight.
  • You suspect a basic problem, such as light, sound, frame or slate, but cannot see it yourself.
  • You cannot fit a session into your week, or would rather have it in writing to come back to.

What written notes cannot do

They cannot ask you a question back. They cannot watch you try something new. They cannot know you or the project. Questions to ask before you book any coach

  • What will I leave the session with?
  • Will you watch my actual tape, or only talk about it?
  • Do you promise results? Be careful of anyone who promises you work.
  • What does it cost, and what is the cancellation rule?

Coaches set their own rates and they vary, so ask before you book. This page names none.

Using both

Plenty of actors use both: a coach or a class for the scene, and written notes on the tape for the basics before it goes out. Neither one replaces rehearsal. Neither one can do the acting for you.
