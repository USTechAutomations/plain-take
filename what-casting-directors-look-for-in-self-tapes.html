What are casting directors looking for in self-tapes?

This page cannot tell you what any casting director is looking for. Nobody outside a casting room can speak for the people inside it, and they differ from one project to the next. What it can do is describe what is visible in the first ten seconds of any tape, because that is what anyone gets when they press play, before they have heard a word of your acting.

Start with the request

The most reliable source is the casting notice itself. It may give the slate format, the file type, the length, the framing, even the name to put on the file. Follow it before you follow any general advice, including this page.

Light

Can your face be seen clearly? A face lit from the front, with a small bright point in each eye, is visible. A face with a window behind it is a shape. This is the quickest thing to fix and one of the first things anyone sees.

Sound

Can every word be heard, with nothing hiding it? A hum, an echo, a reader louder than you, or a microphone too far away all get between your voice and the person listening. Listen to your first ten seconds with headphones on.

Frame

Is your face where it should be? Chest-up, a little head room, eyes about a third of the way down, and your eyeline just beside the lens. If the picture is off, the eye goes to the picture instead of you. There is more in the framing and eyeline guide.

Slate

Do they know who you are and what you are reading for? Say your name and whatever else the notice asks for, plainly, in the light, with your eyes already where the scene needs them. Do not rush it, and do not perform it. See how to slate a self-tape.

A clear first choice

When the scene begins, is it clear what you want from the other person? It does not need to be big. It can show in how you listen to the first cue line and how you begin. A choice you can see in the first few seconds gives everything after it something to build on. A blank start leaves the watcher waiting to find out what the scene is.

A ten-second test

Play the first ten seconds of your tape twice. First with the sound off: can you see a face, and tell who is speaking? Then with your eyes closed: can you catch every word? If both pass, the basics are not standing in the way.

That is all these ten seconds can do. They cannot make anyone cast you, and this page does not claim to know what will. They remove obstacles, so that your acting is the thing that gets watched.

What to do with the rest of the tape

The rest is craft: listening, specific choices, and staying with the scene. Those are worth getting a person's eye on when you can, such as a coach, a class or a trusted scene partner. See coach or written notes for how to choose.
