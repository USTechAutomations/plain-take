Common self-tape mistakes and how to fix each one

These are the mistakes that turn up on tape after tape, not because anyone is careless, but because a home setup hides them until you actually look. Each one below has a plain fix, not a lecture.

Rushing the slate

The mistake: saying your name and the role fast, then taking a breath before the scene actually starts, as if the slate does not count.

The fix: treat the slate as the first few seconds of the tape, not a chore before it. Say it at the same energy and pace you would use meeting someone in person.

Sitting too far from the microphone

The mistake: recording on a laptop mic from a normal talking distance, so the room sound and any background hum end up louder than you expect.

The fix: get any microphone closer than the laptop's built-in one, even a phone propped just out of frame, and test it with headphones before the real take.

Saving your energy for later

The mistake: playing the first half of a scene flat because the strongest moment is near the end, so the tape opens with nothing for a viewer to hold onto.

The fix: commit to a real choice from the first line. A viewer who is not engaged in the first ten seconds may not get to your best moment at all.

Cutting off the top of your head

The mistake: framing so close, or sitting so near the camera, that the top of your head is out of frame for part or all of the tape.

The fix: leave a little room above your head and check the frame again after you sit down in your actual position, not from where you set up the camera.

A busy or distracting background

The mistake: a bookshelf, a window with people passing, or a bright poster behind you that pulls a viewer's eye away from your face.

The fix: pick the plainest wall or corner in your home, even if it means moving to a closet or a hallway for a few minutes.

Skipping the role name in the slate

The mistake: saying your name and stopping there, leaving out which role or which side you are reading, especially when auditioning for more than one part.

The fix: always name the role after your name unless the casting notice specifically says not to.

Sending the wrong file format

The mistake: uploading a file type or a file name that does not match what the breakdown asked for, which can slow down or block the upload entirely.

The fix: read the casting notice's technical instructions the same way you read the sides, and match the file name and format exactly.

Sending it without watching it back

The mistake: recording a take that feels right in the moment and sending it straight away, without checking how it actually reads on screen.

The fix: watch the full tape once, at normal volume, before you send it. A minute of watching catches almost everything else on this list.
