How to slate a self-tape

A slate is the few seconds at the start of a self-tape where you say who you are before the scene begins. It sounds small, but it is often the first thing anyone sees of you, so it is worth doing on purpose rather than as an afterthought.

What a slate is for

A slate tells whoever is watching who you are and, often, which role you are reading for, before the scene starts. It is not a performance moment. It is information, delivered plainly.

Think of it the way you would introduce yourself walking into a room: clear, brief, and in the light, not rushed and half-turned away.

The usual order of information

Most of the time, a slate goes: your name, then the role you are reading for. Some breakdowns also ask for your location, your height, or your representation, in which case say those in the order the notice lists them.

If you are reading more than one role or more than one side, say which one is coming up before each new take, so nobody has to guess.

Tone

Say your slate at a normal, friendly pace, the way you would say it meeting a director in person. It does not need energy borrowed from the scene, and it does not need to be quiet or apologetic either.

Keep your eyes and body already in the position you will use for the scene, so the slate is not a separate, distracted moment before the real thing starts.

When to follow the casting notice instead

If a breakdown gives specific slate instructions, such as skipping the slate entirely, using only your first name, or including a specific detail, follow that over any general habit.

Casting notices vary by project, by casting director, and sometimes by role, so re-read the instructions each time rather than assuming last week's format still applies.

What to do with no instructions at all

When a notice says nothing about slating, the safe default is name, then role, said plainly, in the light, before the scene begins.

If you are unsure whether to slate at all, including a short one rarely hurts, while leaving one out when it was expected can read as a missed instruction.

Slating between multiple sides

If you are taping more than one scene or more than one role in the same session, slate again before each new one so whoever watches later does not have to guess which side is which.

A short pause and a fresh slate between sides takes a few seconds and saves confusion for anyone reviewing several tapes back to back.

Common slate mistakes

Saying the slate after the scene instead of before, leaning out of the light while you say your name, or forgetting the role entirely are the three that turn up most often.

Recording the slate as its own short clip first, before you run the scene, can help you catch these before they become a habit.
